04

Scheduling rules

The generator enforces these. A row that breaks one is a bug.

  1. Every market gets all five event types every month, once each. Eventbrite calendar: 195 × 5 × 12 = 11,700 in the US; 25 × 5 × 12 = 1,500 in Canada. JobFairX calendar: 349 × 5 × 12 = 20,940 and 60 × 5 × 12 = 3,600.
  2. Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday only, 11:00 to 15:00 local. Never a Monday, Friday or weekend.
  3. One event type per week, the same type everywhere. A four-week month runs week 1 Veterans, week 2 Entry-Level, week 3 Healthcare, week 4 Technology and Diversity together. A three-week month has no week 4: Healthcare joins Veterans in week 1, week 2 stays Entry-Level only, and Technology and Diversity close the month. A five-week month runs one type a week in that order. types_this_week says how many types run in a week. The one row outside this pattern is Eugene, OR's Veterans event of February 9 on the JobFairX calendar (§01).
  4. March is the one exception. Its last week is March 30–31 only, and March 31 is Cesar Chavez Day, when California's events must move. Two types on two days would leave California nowhere to move to, so in March Technology and Diversity run in week 3 (March 24–25) and Healthcare in week 4 (March 30–31).
  5. Each week's markets are spread across its usable days: on the Eventbrite calendar about 65 US events a day in a one-type week with three open days, about 130 in a two-type week; on the JobFairX calendar about 116 and 233. A two-type week with only two open days (January 12–14 has three; April 20–21, March 24–25 and December 1–2 have two) puts an event in every market on both days.
  6. A market's weekday rotates month to month (Austin: 20 Tuesdays, 21 Wednesdays, 19 Thursdays). Each market has a fixed place in the rotation (rotation in the market files), so adding or removing a market never moves another market's dates. On the JobFairX calendar, the 188 markets that are on the website but not on the Eventbrite calendar have the places that give the dates the website already shows. Youngstown, OH is new and takes the next free place.
  7. A market never has two events on one day.
  8. Closed windows. US: July 5–9, November 22–28, December 18–31. Canada: July 1–2, October 11–15, December 18–31.
  9. No events on a blocked observance in jobfairx-2027-holidays.csv.
  10. A week with only one usable day is skipped. Weeks are cut at month edges, so a lone day at a month's edge is skipped too.

05

Holiday policy

All 44 US federal and religious observances in 2027 are hard blocks; only 11 of them fall on a Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday. “No events on any religious holiday” cannot be applied literally, because nearly every date touches some observance (every day of Ramadan, for example). An independent audit of 617 observances produced the tiers below.

April 21 is the eve of Passover; the first Seder is at sundown. Events stay, because the 11:00–15:00 window ends well before it, and each carries an informational note.

Tier B (a large community affected, nothing closes; not acted on): April 14 Vaisakhi, June 15 Ashura, September 15 Mid-Autumn Festival and Chuseok, September 16 Mexican Independence Day, October 7 Durga Ashtami, October 28 Deepavali (South Indian date), December 8 Immaculate Conception. Tier C (observed, a normal working day) is ignored.

Canada drops the US federal days and closes July 1–2, September 30, October 11–15, November 11 and December 18–31 nationally. June 24 (Fête nationale) applies to Quebec only. No Quebec market is on the Eventbrite calendar; Gatineau is on the JobFairX calendar and has no event that day. Every other Canadian statutory holiday in 2027 falls on a Monday.

Estimated dates. Islamic dates (Eid al-Fitr March 9–10, Eid al-Adha May 16–17) depend on moon sighting, and Hindu dates (Holi March 22, Diwali October 29) need astronomical confirmation. Re-check them in early 2027; a one-day shift of Eid al-Fitr can change a March week.

Integration notes

  • Labels match the live stickers exactly: Veterans, Entry-Level, Healthcare, Technology, Diversity.
  • One calendar per table. The JobFairX files already contain every Eventbrite calendar row, with the same event_id. The website uses the JobFairX files; the Eventbrite files only say what goes on Eventbrite.
  • Eventbrite gets four of the five types. Leave every Diversity row off Eventbrite; load the rest of the Eventbrite calendar (§01).
  • Dates are local. Store date as a date, not a UTC timestamp, or events can drift a day.
  • Time zones. Every event runs 11:00–15:00 in the market's own time, and the files have no time-zone column: resolve it per market from the ZIP or postal code. States that span two zones (TX, FL, TN, KY, IN, ND, SD, NE, KS, OR, ID) resolve per city. British Columbia stopped changing its clocks in 2026 and stays on UTC−7 all year, so its winter events are not on Pacific Standard Time. Saskatchewan has no daylight saving time.
  • Import key. Use event_id. Because it contains the date, a file regenerated after a schedule change is not a clean upsert: remove the affected markets' old 2027 rows first.
  • Early registration closes on early_reg_deadline, 30 days before the event.
  • Area names. Inland Empire, Long Island and Orange County are areas, not cities; they have past sales and stay. Nepean, ON is inside Ottawa, which is also listed; it is flagged (review_flag) and scheduled. The JobFairX calendar lists every city as the website has it, so it also has Okanagan, BC (a region), Kanata, ON (inside Ottawa), Sherwood Park, AB (a hamlet), Dartmouth, NS (inside Halifax), Clinton, MI (read as Clinton Charter Township) and Redwood, CA (no city of that name; no population or ZIP code).
