Scottsdale Technology event page, rezi.ai direction: build notes for Brandon

Design reference for jobfairx.com/employer/job-fairs/arizona/scottsdale/next-technology (the employer event-page template), rebuilt in the same system as the rezi-direction employer home page: rezi.ai's layout and feel, JobFairX colours, Inter. Every word is the live event page's copy, the live FAQ, or the live home page's product wording, except the hero headline and supporting line, which come from Scott's 22 Oct brief. Design only: nothing here is production code, and nothing here changes the event data model. Product screens are the prototype captures used on the home page (a Dallas demo event); the live page shows this event's own data in the same frames.

The event-type switch (review aid, far left edge)

The small T / E / D / V / H control on the left edge of the page switches the whole design between the five live employer event pages, so one link shows every type: T Technology (Scottsdale, Oct 29), E Entry-Level (Dallas, Nov 18), D Diversity (Birmingham, Dec 17), V Veterans (Dearborn, Nov 2), H Healthcare (Boise, Oct 28). It sits in the page margin on desktop (the content column starts further right) and becomes a small row at the bottom-left on phones. It is a review aid, not part of the production page. Each type can be linked directly with a hash: #technology, #entry-level, #diversity, #veteran, #healthcare; the last choice is remembered in the browser.

What changes per type, all read from that type's live page on 22 Oct 2026: the event pill, date, time and timezone; the registration countdown; the H1 (the live headline with the city added: "Hire emerging new talent in Dallas.", "Build a more inclusive team in Birmingham.", "Hire mission-ready veterans in Dearborn.", "Fill your open healthcare roles in Boise."; Technology keeps the brief's line); the supporting line (the live sub copy; Technology keeps the brief's); the pre-registered count and the three averages with their caption; the past-company logos (each type's own set from its live page, in img/past/ with a type prefix); the results heading, sub-line and three testimonials; the messaging mock's job title, the Schedule name and message, and the Waiting-for-response rows; the closing headline, event card, page title and meta description. Everything else (steps, interview management, pricing, FAQ, contact, footer) is identical across types on the live pages too. Live pages: Technology · Entry-Level · Diversity · Veterans · Healthcare.

What stays exactly as it is on the live page

  • Event facts: Scottsdale, AZ · Technology Hiring Event · October 29, 2026 · 11:00 AM – 3:00 PM MST. The date, time and timezone come from the event record, never from the template.
  • Dynamic values, marked in the markup with data-dynamic: the pre-registered candidate count (pre-registered-count, shown twice: the proof strip and the closing card) and the registration countdown (registration-countdown, "Employer registration closes in N days", the live top bar's text). Both keep their live source. The three historical figures (53 average interviews, 11 average hires, 84% show rate) are the technology type's figures from the live page and are captioned "Averages across past JobFairX technology hiring events" so they are never read as this event's promise.
  • Reserve My Spot: every button carries data-live="reserve" and must call the live page's existing Reserve handler (checkout). In this reference they scroll to the closing band. Labels are identical everywhere: Reserve My Spot.
  • Pricing: Starter $495, Growth $895 (Most Popular, as on the live page), Pro $1,495, the four lines per package, All packages include, and the bundles callout with its link to /employer/hiring-event-bundles. Any promotional pricing logic stays in the component.
  • FAQ: the six live questions and answers verbatim, as real details/summary elements so the answers are in the HTML (the live page injects them on click, which search engines never see; worth fixing while here). FAQPage JSON-LD stays as it is.
  • Testimonials: James K., Priya D. and Ryan T., exact words and attributions. The Unsplash portraits are replaced with initials because stock faces presented as the person misrepresent who said it.
  • Past companies: the sixteen logo files already on the page (Accenture to VMware). The scrolling strip of CloudScale, ByteForge Labs, NexGen and the other invented names is not carried over (flagged to Scott on 30 Sep 2026).
  • Contact form: the five live fields, Submit, reCAPTCHA notice. Title, meta description and canonical as live. Event JSON-LD unchanged by this design; note separately that it declares an online-only event while the page offers in-person interviews.
  • Nav, footer, Sign In (virtual.jobfairx.com) and all page links as live.

System

Identical tokens, type, buttons, frames, reveals and the pinned section to the employer home page in this folder (employer-home-rezi.html); its build notes carry the measured rezi values and the motion rules. Page-specific CSS is appended at the end of the stylesheet under "event page additions". Breakpoints: 1100px (logo grid 4 across, flow panel 2 + 1), 900px (proof strip stacks, walkthrough stacks, points stack, contact stacks), 640px (phone sizes). Reduced motion: no reveals, no pin, no count-up, no tab cycling.

Decisions for Scott

  1. Headline: "Meet your next great tech hire in Scottsdale." (the brief's line with the city added so the H1 says where). The live H1 was "Hire Top Tech Talent".
  2. The invented company strip is out; the sixteen real logos carry the proof.
  3. Testimonial portraits replaced with initials.
  4. The YouTube walkthrough is a poster card linking out rather than an embedded player.
  5. Reserve My Spot scrolls to the closing band in this reference; Brandon wires the live handler.
