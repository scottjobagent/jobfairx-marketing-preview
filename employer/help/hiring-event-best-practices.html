Hiring event best practices

The habits that separate a full interview schedule from a half-empty one. Every suggestion here comes back to something you can actually set or do in the platform.

Setting up

Set capacity to what you can staff, not what you hope to fill

Every interview a time slot allows is a slot a candidate can book. If you set four interviews in the 11:00 slot, four people can arrive at 11:00 expecting to be seen, and you need four interviewers signed in at that moment. The platform does the arithmetic for you and warns when a slot needs more people than you are likely to have — that warning is worth reading rather than scrolling past.

Where you cannot cover a period, set those slots to zero. A closed slot cannot be booked, which is far better than a booked interview nobody turns up to run. See Interview details.

Choose the interview format deliberately

The format is an event-level setting: every candidate who requests an interview arrives on it, and it can't be changed once you publish your jobs. Rescheduling a single interview is the only way to differ from it, so decide before you publish. See Choosing your interview location.

  • Video on a JobFairX video call is the least friction for a candidate — nothing to download and no link to manage.
  • Phone puts the work on you: you call each candidate at their scheduled time, so build in a moment between calls.
  • In person needs the address and the How to attend box filled in properly, because both go into every candidate's confirmation. Vague directions are a reliable way to create no-shows.

Add your interviewers early

Adding someone emails them a link straight into the event's lobby. Doing it a week out rather than the morning of gives them time to find the email, follow the link, and check their setup works. See Adding interviewers.

How many you can add depends on your package: 2 on Starter, 5 on Growth, and unlimited on Pro. Your own account is one of them, so a Starter event is you plus one colleague.

Decide auto-accept on purpose

Auto-accept confirms candidates the moment they pick a slot. It fills a schedule faster and spares people a wait, and it pauses on its own once every slot is full. The trade is that you have not looked at anyone before they are booked. Turn it on when volume matters more than screening, and leave it off when you want to read a resume first.

In the run-up

Answer requests quickly

Every request sitting in Pending is somebody deciding whether you are worth holding the morning for. Requests that go unanswered for days tend to become no-shows even when you do eventually accept them. Make it a daily habit rather than a job for the day before.

Reschedule rather than decline when the time is the only problem

Decline ends the conversation. If the person looks right and the slot is wrong, propose a different time instead — you can offer more than one, including times outside the event's scheduled hours or date, and change the format for that one interview if that is what makes it work. The request then reads Proposed · awaiting candidate until they choose.

Turn on the automations that match how you already work

The five presets cover the moments people most often forget: a new request arriving, an interview being scheduled, a request declined, an interview missed, and an interview completed. Switching on even one or two removes a batch of manual follow-up. Do not switch on all five and then write nothing personal — a generic message at every step reads worse than silence at some of them.

Have everyone test audio and video

The dashboard carries a Test your audio and video before your event link, and it exists because corporate IT often blocks camera and microphone access by default. Run it once per person, on the machine and network they will actually use, far enough ahead that IT can act if something is blocked.

The morning of is too late

Audio and video permissions, interviewer invitations, and anything IT has to approve all need lead time. A candidate waiting in a room while somebody hunts for a working microphone is the most avoidable bad experience on the platform.

On the day

Keep your status honest

On a video event your status drives whether candidates are called forward. Set it to Available when you are ready and back to Away whenever you step out, even briefly. Leaving it on Available while you are away from the desk is how somebody ends up sitting in a room alone.

Watch the waiting room, not just the interview rooms

Candidates ready to be seen appear in Interview rooms, but the queue behind them sits in Waiting rooms with a wait time against each name. That column is your early warning that you are falling behind, well before anyone gives up.

Use the right answer when a call ends badly

Ending an interview asks whether you have finished with that person. Answering No returns them to the front of the interview line so they can join again — which is what you want when a call drops, when the connection is unusable, or when a colleague should speak to them instead. Answering Yes marks them interviewed and they leave the queue.

Record the rating while it is fresh

You are asked for a recommendation as soon as the call ends. Answer it then. Ratings left for later become Needs review rows nobody can reconstruct a week on, and the rating is the only outcome measure JobFairX keeps. Notes taken during the interview are worth more than notes written from memory afterwards.

After the event

Clear Needs review the same week

The Needs review filter on the event report shows interviews that happened but were never rated. Every one of them is a candidate in limbo and a hole in your numbers. Clearing them is usually twenty minutes of work and it makes everything else on the report trustworthy.

Work the Yes list first, and do it quickly

Filter the report to Yes and move on those people before anyone else does. The row menu offers Schedule Interview and Send a Message without leaving the report, so a second conversation can be booked in a few seconds.

Compare events rather than judging one in isolation

A single event tells you very little. The Events table in Analytics puts them side by side, and the interesting comparison is usually requests against completed — a big gap means people booked and did not arrive, which is a reminder or an expectations problem, not a sourcing one.

Pre-event checklist

Worth running through a week out. The interview format and slot length are not on it, because both are locked once you publish your jobs.

One thing to remember on the day

Editing closes when an event starts. Jobs and slot capacity are fixed from that moment, so anything on the checklist above has to be settled beforehand. The interview format and slot length are fixed earlier, when you publish your jobs. What stays available during a live event is rescheduling and cancelling individual interviews.
