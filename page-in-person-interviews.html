The part that gets misread

In person isn’t a kind of event. It’s where your interviews happen.

An event

A market moment.

The Dallas Healthcare Hiring Event: one city, one date, one window. Every employer who registers joins that same event. There is no in-person version of it and no virtual version of it, because the event doesn’t carry a setting for your interviews — you do.

An interview location

Yours, and yours alone.

You set it during setup, for your own interviews, at each event you join: JobFairX video, phone, in person at your address, or your own link. Two employers at the same Dallas Healthcare event can interview completely differently — one on video, one with candidates walking into their office.

How it works

Three steps, all of them during setup.

1

Choose in person.

Interview location is part of setting up the event, alongside your jobs and your interviewer seats. Pick In person and the row on your events dashboard changes with it.

2

Enter your address.

The street address where you want to meet people — your office, a branch, a space you’ve booked. That address becomes the interview location for that event.

3

Candidates get it, then get reminded.

It lands on their confirmation and on every reminder after it. Candidates already receive automated email and SMS in the run-up to a scheduled interview; your address rides along on all of them.

Nothing else moves. Candidates still request interviews, you still review résumés before deciding, and accepting a request is what schedules the interview — exactly as it works for a video or phone event. The only thing that changed is the answer to “where”.

What candidates are told

Candidates receive pre-event preparation materials, interview tips and guidance on presenting themselves professionally, plus automated emails and SMS leading up to their scheduled interview. Where the interview happens is part of that from the moment you accept.

They are being asked to travel a short, verified distance to an address in the city they already live in — not to work out a video link ten minutes before it starts.

When to use it

When the room is the point.

Most interviews don’t need one. Some can’t do without — and those are the roles that have been hardest to hire through a screen.

A walkthrough

The floor, the ward, the line, the loading bay. Some roles are far easier to accept once someone has stood in the place they’d be standing daily.

A skills test

Anything you need to watch someone actually do, with your own eyes and your own tools rather than a description of it.

Equipment

Machinery, vehicles, instruments, a kitchen. Things that don’t fit down a video call and don’t travel to one either.

A handshake

Roles where the first meeting decides it, and both sides would rather it happened in a room.

Everything else

The rest of the event is unchanged.

  • Same matching

    Candidate matching activates immediately once your jobs are posted, and interview requests start arriving within a few hours.

  • Same review, same decision

    Résumés and contact details for every matched candidate. You accept or decline each request, or switch on automatic scheduling to accept them for you.

  • Same dashboard

    Messaging, automations, interviewer seats, dispositions, notes and analytics behave exactly as they do on a video event.

  • Same post-event report

    Recruiter notes, interview records and résumés afterwards — and access that doesn’t expire, so follow-ups keep running through the platform.
